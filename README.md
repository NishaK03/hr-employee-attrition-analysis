# HR Employee Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyses employee attrition using the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset. It demonstrates how Business Analysis techniques and data analytics can be used to investigate workforce trends, identify potential attrition-related factors, and develop evidence-based recommendations.

TechNova Pvt Ltd is used as a hypothetical business scenario for this portfolio case study. It is not the organisation that produced the source dataset.

## Business Problem

Employee attrition can increase recruitment and onboarding costs, disrupt team continuity, and affect organisational productivity. HR stakeholders need a structured way to understand attrition patterns and identify areas requiring further investigation.

The objective of this project is to analyse employee attrition across departments, compensation, overtime, job satisfaction, work-life balance, business travel, and other available workforce attributes.

## Project Objectives

- Calculate the overall employee attrition rate.
- Compare attrition across departments and employee groups.
- Examine relationships between attrition and compensation, overtime, satisfaction, and career progression.
- Translate findings into actionable recommendations for HR stakeholders.
- Design an interactive dashboard to communicate key metrics and patterns.

## Dataset

- **Source:** IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset
- **Records:** 1,470 employees
- **Target variable:** Attrition
- **Data type:** Sample employee HR data

The dataset is used for analytical and educational purposes. The hypothetical TechNova scenario does not represent a verified real-world client engagement.

## Tools

- Microsoft Excel — exploratory data analysis and Pivot Tables
- SQL — querying, aggregation, and attrition-rate calculations
- Microsoft Power BI — the dashboard file was developed 

## Initial Findings

The preliminary exploratory analysis identified the following patterns:

- Overall recorded attrition rate: 16.12%.
- Sales had the highest department-level attrition rate in the initial analysis.
- Employees who left had lower average monthly income than employees who stayed.
- Attrition rates were higher among employees recorded as working overtime.
- The lowest work-life balance rating group had a higher attrition rate than the overall workforce.

These are descriptive findings from the supplied dataset. They do not establish that any single factor caused employees to leave.

## Limitations

The analysis is based on a sample dataset and does not represent a verified company's current workforce. Results show associations within the dataset and should not be interpreted as causal explanations or individual employee risk predictions.

Financial savings and future attrition reductions have not been validated through an implemented intervention or a controlled evaluation.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates a structured approach to business problem definition, stakeholder analysis, exploratory data analysis, SQL querying, dashboard planning, and business recommendations.

The objective is to translate workforce data into clear, appropriately qualified insights that can support further HR investigation and decision-making.
