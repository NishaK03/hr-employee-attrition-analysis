# HR Employee Attrition Analysis — Stakeholder Register

## 1. Purpose

The stakeholder register identifies the key stakeholders involved in the hypothetical TechNova Pvt Ltd employee attrition analysis project, their responsibilities, their primary concerns, and the information they need to support business decisions.

Understanding stakeholder needs helps ensure that the analysis addresses relevant business problems and presents findings in a format that supports decision-making.

**Note:** TechNova Pvt Ltd is a hypothetical business scenario created for this portfolio project. The stakeholder roles are illustrative.

## 2. Stakeholder Register

| Stakeholder | Role in the Project | Primary Concern | Information Required | Influence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HR Manager | Primary stakeholder | Understanding employee attrition patterns and identifying retention opportunities | Overall attrition rate, department-level comparisons, satisfaction patterns, and potential retention initiatives | High |
| Finance Head | Secondary stakeholder | Understanding the potential financial impact of employee turnover | Recruitment and replacement costs, estimated turnover costs, and assumptions behind proposed savings | High |
| Department Managers | Operational stakeholders | Maintaining team continuity and minimising disruption caused by employee departures | Department-level attrition, overtime patterns, job satisfaction, and career progression indicators | Medium–High |
| CEO | Executive decision-maker | Understanding workforce stability and organisational priorities | Executive summary, key performance indicators, priority findings, risks, and recommendations | High |
| Business Analyst | Analysis and solution enabler | Translating business needs into structured analysis and actionable findings | Business requirements, data quality, KPI definitions, SQL results, dashboard outputs, and documented limitations | Medium |

## 3. Stakeholder Needs

### 3.1 HR Manager

**Primary need:** Understand employee attrition patterns and identify opportunities to improve retention.

Key questions:
- What proportion of employees left?
- Which departments have comparatively higher attrition rates?
- How are overtime, compensation, satisfaction, and work-life balance associated with attrition?
- Which retention initiatives should be investigated first?

**Project deliverable:** An attrition dashboard, supporting analysis, and prioritised recommendations.

### 3.2 Finance Head

**Primary need:** Understand the potential financial implications of employee turnover.

Key questions:
- What costs are associated with recruiting and onboarding replacement employees?
- Which roles are the most expensive to replace?
- What assumptions would be required to estimate potential savings from reduced attrition?

**Project deliverable:** A framework for further cost analysis.

**Data limitation:** The supplied dataset does not contain verified recruitment or replacement costs. Actual financial impact and savings cannot be calculated without additional data.

### 3.3 Department Managers

**Primary need:** Understand workforce patterns that may affect team stability.

Key questions:
- Which departments have comparatively higher attrition rates?
- Is overtime associated with higher attrition in particular employee groups?
- How do job satisfaction and career progression relate to attrition patterns?

**Project deliverable:** Department-level findings and questions for further investigation.

### 3.4 CEO

**Primary need:** Obtain a concise view of workforce stability and potential business priorities.

Key questions:
- What is the overall attrition rate?
- Which findings require management attention?
- What actions should be considered first?
- What additional information is needed before committing resources?

**Project deliverable:** An executive-level summary of key metrics, findings, limitations, and proposed actions.

### 3.5 Business Analyst

**Primary responsibility:** Ensure that the analysis addresses the defined business problem and communicates findings accurately.

Key activities:
- Elicit and document business questions.
- Identify stakeholder information needs.
- Define KPIs and calculation methods.
- Analyse and validate the available data.
- Translate analytical findings into business recommendations.
- Communicate assumptions, risks, and limitations.
- Document the deliverables for review.

**Project deliverable:** Traceable documentation connecting business needs, analysis, findings, and recommendations.

## 4. Stakeholder Prioritisation

Stakeholders are prioritised based on their decision-making authority, involvement in workforce management, and need for analytical outputs.

| Priority | Stakeholder | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| High | HR Manager | Primary user of attrition analysis and retention recommendations |
| High | CEO | Executive decision-maker responsible for strategic priorities |
| High | Finance Head | Required for validating cost assumptions and potential financial benefits |
| Medium–High | Department Managers | Responsible for understanding team-level patterns and evaluating operational changes |
| Medium | Business Analyst | Responsible for analysis and delivery rather than approving business policy |

These priorities are provisional and would be validated during stakeholder discussions in a real project.

## 5. Stakeholder Communication Plan

| Stakeholder | Information to Communicate | Suggested Format | Proposed Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| HR Manager | Attrition metrics, departmental patterns, findings, and recommendations | Interactive dashboard and review meeting | At key project milestones |
| Finance Head | Cost assumptions, required financial inputs, and potential impact assessment | Summary report and discussion | During business-impact analysis |
| Department Managers | Department-level patterns and areas for further investigation | Focused dashboard views and review session | During findings validation |
| CEO | Executive summary, priority findings, risks, and proposed actions | Concise management presentation | At project completion |
| Business Analyst | Requirements, data checks, calculations, and analysis decisions | Working documentation and analysis files | Throughout the project |

The communication formats and frequency are proposed for the case study, not records of actual stakeholder meetings.

## 6. Stakeholder Risks and Considerations

| Risk or Consideration | Potential Impact | Proposed Response |
|---|---|---|
| Stakeholders interpret correlation as causation | Retention actions may target the wrong factors | Explain that observed relationships require further investigation |
| Financial costs are unavailable | Business savings may be overstated | Request verified recruitment, onboarding, and replacement-cost data |
| Different stakeholders define success differently | Project outputs may not meet expectations | Agree on measurable success criteria early |
| Sensitive employee data is mishandled | Privacy and confidentiality concerns | Use appropriate access controls and avoid exposing identifiable employee information |
| Recommendations are implemented without validation | Policies may be ineffective or unfair | Validate findings and pilot proposed interventions before wider implementation |

## 7. Stakeholder Validation

In a real business engagement, the Business Analyst would validate this register with the project sponsor and relevant stakeholders.

Validation would confirm:
- Stakeholder responsibilities and decision-making authority.
- Business priorities and reporting expectations.
- KPI definitions and acceptance criteria.
- Data access and privacy requirements.
- Review milestones and approval responsibilities.

No actual stakeholder interviews or approvals are claimed as part of this portfolio case study.

## 8. Conclusion

The stakeholder register establishes a structured view of the people and functions that would use or influence the employee attrition analysis.

By documenting stakeholder concerns, information needs, communication approaches, and risks, the project connects technical analysis with business decision-making.
